How it works

  1. Terrain. For every 20 m spot in a 2 × 2 km site, MARGEN traces the horizon in 360 directions across NASA LRO LOLA terrain: 20 m detail nearby, 80 m out to 250 km, with the Moon's curvature.
  2. Sky. JPL DE440 ephemerides (NAIF SPICE) give the Sun and Earth every hour from January to mid-April 2027. Sunlight counts the visible part of the Sun's disc; Earth in view needs Earth's center above the terrain. Solar panels and antenna sit 2 m above the ground.
  3. Landings. 1,000 landing points scatter around the aim with a 100 m typical miss. A landing works if, for its first 14 days, sunlight reaches it at least 50% of the time and Earth is in view at least 30% of the time. With 1,000 landings the chance is accurate to ±3.1 points or better (95%).

What it doesn't model

  • Rocks, small craters and slopes smaller than the 20 m map spots. Athena's crater is one of them.
  • Lander tilt, dust, temperature and batteries.
  • Radio links. Earth in view is a clear line to Earth; it does not check antennas or ground stations.
  • Light during the descent.
  • Real landing errors are not the same in every direction. MARGEN uses a 100 m typical miss.
  • Terrain more than 250 km away.
