Each dot is a random spot near the south pole, 2008–2026. On the diagonal, MARGEN and NASA agree.

How this comparison was made
  • 160 random spots between 85°S and the pole, averaged over 18.6 years, a full cycle of the Moon's orbit.
  • Across: NASA's long-term maps (LOLA AVGVISIB, PGDA). Up: MARGEN's method on NASA's 80 m terrain, from the ground.
  • The navigator adds 20 m terrain near each site and a 2 m lander. That finer terrain is not tested here.
  • Earth counts as in view when any part shows, as in NASA's map. The navigator asks for Earth's center.

How it works

  1. TerrainHorizon in 360 directions for every 20 m spot, on NASA LOLA terrain out to 250 km.
  2. SkySun and Earth every hour of 2027 from JPL DE440, seen from 2 m above the ground.
  3. Landings1,000 landings, 100 m typical miss, checked for slope, sunlight and Earth in view.

Not covered

  • Rocks and craters under 20 m
  • Slopes shorter than 40 m
  • Batteries, cold and dust
  • Radio links and ground stations
  • Light during the descent
  • Landing hours other than 00:00 UTC
  • Misses larger than 100 m
  • Terrain past 250 km or north of 80°S
